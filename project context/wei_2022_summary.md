Wei's thesis is the primary source for:

- Long-term cycling stability data

## Chapter Summaries

**Chapter 2 — Ni zeolite 5A and 13X catalysts (effect of Ni precursor)**
- Prepared Ni/zeolite catalysts by evaporation impregnation
- Nickel citrate and acetate → smaller NiO particle sizes than nitrate
- 13X catalysts with nickel citrate showed clearly higher activity
- Conclusion: precursor complex size and zeolite pore size both matter

**Chapter 3 — Ru as promoter for Ni/zeolite catalysts**
- Added Ru to Ni zeolite 13X and 5A catalysts
- Mono-metallic Ni outperformed bi-metallic Ni-Ru (dispersion harder to achieve with two metals)
- 2.5%Ru13X and 5%Ni13X showed good stability: ~97% CH4 selectivity at 360°C over 200 h

**Chapter 4 — Ce-promoted Ni 13X zeolite catalysts**
- Sub-nanometer Ni-Ce on zeolite 13X support
- Ce improves reduction of NiO, increases medium basic sites
- **5%Ni2.5%Ce13X: high activity and ~100% CH4 selectivity at 360°C**
- High metal dispersion attributed to zeolite selectivity for metal citrate complexes

**Chapter 5 — Sorption-enhanced CO2 methanation with 5%Ni2.5%Ce13X**
- Fixed-bed SEM experiments with bi-functional material (catalytic + water adsorption)
- 100 reactive adsorption–desorption cycles, 203 hours total
- Near-100% CO2 conversion and CH4 selectivity maintained
- Slight decrease in water uptake capacity at low temperature only
- Performance ranking: 5%Ni2.5%Ce13X > 5%Ni13X > 5%Ni5A > 5%NiL

**Chapter 6 — Kinetics of Ni/zeolite 13X vs Ni/γ-Al2O3**
- LHHW rate model derived and validated
- 13X zeolite support more active than γ-Al2O3 (better Ni dispersion)
- Methane partial pressure has no influence on CO2 methanation kinetics
- Main difference between supports: water partial pressure exponent in rate expression
